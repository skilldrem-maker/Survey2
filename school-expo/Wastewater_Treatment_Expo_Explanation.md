# Wastewater Treatment Plant — School Expo Explanation

## Introduction

Good morning everyone.

Our project demonstrates how wastewater is treated in a wastewater treatment plant. Wastewater contains different types of pollutants, such as large solid waste, sand, suspended particles, organic matter, and harmful microorganisms. Instead of releasing this water directly into the environment, it goes through several treatment stages.

## 1. Screening

First, the wastewater enters the screening unit.

Screens remove large materials such as plastic, cloth, paper, sticks, and other large objects.

This protects the pumps and other equipment from blockage and damage.

## 2. Grit Removal

After screening, the wastewater enters the grit removal unit.

Grit means heavy inorganic particles such as sand, small stones, and other dense particles.

The water flow is controlled so that these heavy particles settle at the bottom due to gravity.

The collected grit is then removed.

## 3. Primary Sedimentation

Next, the water enters the primary sedimentation tank.

Here, the water moves slowly, allowing suspended solids to settle at the bottom.

The settled material is called primary sludge. Some floating material can also be removed from the surface.

As a result, the water becomes considerably clearer.

## 4. Biological Treatment and Aeration

The water then enters the biological treatment stage.

Microorganisms help break down biodegradable organic matter present in the wastewater.

Air is supplied through an aeration system. The oxygen supports aerobic microorganisms, which consume the organic pollutants.

The bubbles visible in our model represent the aeration process.

## 5. Secondary Sedimentation

After biological treatment, the water contains microorganisms and biological solids.

Therefore, it enters the secondary sedimentation tank.

The biological solids settle at the bottom, while clearer water flows from the top.

Some of the settled sludge can be returned to the biological treatment stage, while excess sludge is sent for sludge treatment.

## 6. Tertiary Treatment

The water then goes through tertiary treatment.

This is an additional treatment stage used when higher water quality is required.

Different technologies can be used depending on the purpose. For example, filtration can remove remaining fine particles.

This stage further improves the quality of the treated water.

## 7. Disinfection

Even after the water becomes clear, microorganisms may still be present.

Therefore, the water undergoes disinfection.

Methods such as UV, chlorine, or ozone can be used to reduce harmful microorganisms.

## 8. Sludge Treatment

The treatment process also produces sludge.

The sludge collected from the primary and secondary treatment stages is processed separately.

Depending on the plant, sludge can be thickened, digested, dewatered, and then safely handled or reused where appropriate.

## 9. Treated Water — Discharge or Reuse

Finally, the treated water can either be discharged according to the required standards or reused for suitable non-drinking purposes, depending on its quality.

## Conclusion

So, the main process is:

Wastewater → Screening → Grit Removal → Primary Sedimentation → Biological Treatment and Aeration → Secondary Sedimentation → Tertiary Treatment → Disinfection → Treated Water

At the same time, the sludge collected during treatment goes through a separate sludge-treatment process.

This process helps protect water resources, reduce pollution, and make it possible to safely reuse water.
