# IMO Class 9 — Formula and Concept Sheet
Use this after learning each concept, not as a substitute for working through the question banks.

## 1. Number Systems
- ((a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2)
- ((sqrt a+sqrt b)(sqrt a-sqrt b)=a-b)
- Rationalise a denominator using its conjugate.
- A rational number in lowest terms has a terminating decimal iff its denominator has only prime factors 2 and/or 5; otherwise its decimal repeats.
- A rational number plus an irrational number is irrational. The sum/product of two irrationals is not always irrational.

## 2. Polynomials
- Remainder theorem: remainder on division by (x-a) is (p(a)).
- Factor theorem: (x-a) is a factor iff (p(a)=0).
- For (ax^2+bx+c) with roots (alpha,eta): (alpha+eta=-b/a), (alphaeta=c/a).
- Degree is the greatest exponent with a nonzero coefficient.

## 3. Algebraic Identities
- ((a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2)
- ((a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2)
- (a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b))

## 4. Coordinate Geometry
- Ordered pair is ((x,y)): x is abscissa, y is ordinate.
- Quadrants: I ((+,+)); II ((-,+)); III ((-,-)); IV ((+,-)).
- On x-axis, (y=0); on y-axis, (x=0); origin ((0,0)).

## 5. Linear Equations in Two Variables
- A linear equation (ax+by+c=0) generally graphs as a straight line.
- Every point on its graph is a solution, and each solution lies on the graph.
- x-intercept: set (y=0). y-intercept: set (x=0).
- (x=k) is vertical; (y=k) is horizontal.

## 6. Euclid's Geometry
- Axiom/common notion: general accepted fact; postulate: accepted starting statement in geometry; theorem: must be proved.
- A diagram supports understanding but is not a proof.
- Two distinct points determine exactly one straight line.

## 7. Lines and Angles
- Complementary angles sum to (90^circ); supplementary angles sum to (180^circ).
- Linear pair sums to (180^circ); vertically opposite angles are equal.
- Parallel lines cut by a transversal: corresponding angles equal; alternate interior angles equal; co-interior angles sum to (180^circ).
- Angles around a point sum to (360^circ).

## 9. Triangles
- Interior angles sum to (180^circ); an exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.
- Triangle inequality: sum of any two sides must exceed the third.
- Larger angle faces the longer side.
- Congruence criteria: SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS.
- Isosceles triangle: equal sides have equal opposite angles.

## 10. Quadrilaterals
- Interior angle sum (360^circ).
- Parallelogram: opposite sides equal/parallel; opposite angles equal; diagonals bisect each other.
- Rectangle: all angles (90^circ), diagonals equal.
- Rhombus: all sides equal; diagonals perpendicular and bisect angles.
- Square has properties of both rectangle and rhombus.

## 11. Areas of Parallelograms and Triangles
- Parallelogram area (=bh).
- Triangle area (=rac12 bh).
- Same base and same parallels: parallelograms have equal areas; a triangle has half the area of the corresponding parallelogram.
- Changing base and height by factors (m,n) changes area by factor (mn).

## 12. Circles
- Circumference (=2pi r=pi d); area (=pi r^2).
- Diameter is the longest chord.
- Perpendicular from centre to a chord bisects the chord.
- Equal chords in the same circle are equidistant from the centre and subtend equal central angles.
- Radius is perpendicular to tangent at the point of contact.

## 13. Area and Perimeter
- Rectangle: area (=lw), perimeter (=2(l+w)).
- Square: area (=s^2), perimeter (=4s).
- Triangle perimeter (=a+b+c).
- Circle: area scales as (r^2), circumference scales as (r).
- Keep length units and area units distinct.

## 14. Constructions
- Perpendicular bisector: use equal-radius arcs from both endpoints, with radius greater than half the segment.
- Angle bisector: construct equal arcs from the two arms, then intersect arcs.
- For SSS construction, the triangle inequality must hold.
- Compass-and-straightedge constructions are based on equal lengths and intersections, not visual guessing.

## 15. Surface Areas and Volumes
- Cube: volume (a^3), total surface area (6a^2), lateral surface area (4a^2).
- Cuboid: volume (lbh), total surface area (2(lb+bh+hl)).
- Cylinder: volume (pi r^2h), curved surface area (2pi rh), total surface area (2pi r(h+r)).
- If every linear dimension scales by (k), area scales by (k^2) and volume by (k^3).
