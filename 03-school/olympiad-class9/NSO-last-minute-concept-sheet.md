## 6. Matter in Our Surroundings
- Particles have spaces, are in constant motion, and attract one another.
- Diffusion is generally fastest in gases and becomes faster as temperature rises.
- Melting/freezing and boiling/condensation are changes of state; during a pure substance's phase change, temperature remains constant while latent heat is absorbed/released.
- Evaporation causes cooling because higher-energy particles escape from the surface.
- Kelvin conversion: (K=^circ C+273) (approximately).
- Evaporation increases with larger surface area, higher temperature, lower humidity and higher wind speed.

## 7. Is Matter Around Us Pure?
- Element: one type of atom; compound: elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio; mixture: substances physically combined.
- Solution: homogeneous; colloid: appears uniform but scatters light; suspension: heterogeneous and particles may settle.
- Tyndall effect is characteristic of colloids, not ordinary true solutions.
- Filtration separates insoluble solids from liquids; evaporation/crystallisation can recover dissolved solids; distillation separates a solvent from a solution; chromatography separates components such as dyes.
- Centrifugation separates components with different densities; separating method depends on physical properties.

## 9. Structure of the Atom
- Atomic number (Z=) number of protons; for a neutral atom, electrons = protons.
- Mass number (A=	ext{protons}+	ext{neutrons}); neutrons (=A-Z).
- Isotopes: same atomic number, different mass numbers. Isobars: same mass number, different atomic numbers.
- In the basic shell model, maximum electrons per shell is (2n^2); first shell holds 2, second holds 8.
- For the first 20 elements, electron shell arrangements are commonly written 2,8,8,2 as appropriate.
- Protons are positive, electrons negative, neutrons neutral.

## 10. Cell — The Fundamental Unit of Life
- Cell membrane is selectively permeable; cell wall gives many plant cells support and protection.
- Nucleus stores most hereditary material in typical eukaryotic cells.
- Mitochondria produce much of the ATP used in cellular processes; ribosomes synthesise proteins; Golgi apparatus modifies/packages materials; lysosomes digest cellular waste.
- Chloroplasts carry out photosynthesis in photosynthetic plant cells.
- Osmosis is movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane; water movement depends on water potential/concentration differences.

## 11. Tissues
- Meristematic tissue divides; apical meristem increases length; lateral meristem contributes to thickness.
- Xylem transports water/minerals; phloem transports prepared food.
- Epithelial tissue covers/protects; connective tissue supports/joins; muscular tissue contracts; nervous tissue transmits impulses.
- Tendon connects muscle to bone; ligament connects bone to bone.
- Guard cells regulate stomatal opening, affecting gas exchange and water loss.

## 12. Diversity in Living Organisms
- Classification organises organisms by shared features and evolutionary relationships.
- Binomial nomenclature uses genus + specific epithet; genus begins with a capital letter, specific epithet lowercase in standard formatting.
- Prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus; eukaryotes have one.
- Vertebrates have a vertebral column; mammals have mammary glands; birds have feathers; amphibians typically use aquatic and terrestrial habitats during their life cycle.
- Fungi are heterotrophic and commonly have cell walls containing chitin; plants typically have cellulose cell walls.

## 13. Human Health and Diseases
- Pathogens cause infectious disease; vectors transmit pathogens between hosts.
- Antibiotics act against bacteria, not ordinary viral infections. Use medicines only as directed by a qualified clinician.
- Vaccines prepare immune memory to recognise particular pathogens/antigens.
- Balanced diet includes appropriate carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water and fibre.
- Deficiency examples: vitamin C—scurvy; vitamin D—rickets; iodine—goitre; vitamin A—night blindness.
- Prevention often includes safe water, sanitation, hand hygiene, vaccination and vector control.

## 14. Improvement in Food Resources
- Crop variety improvement can target yield, quality, disease resistance and tolerance to environmental stress.
- Manure adds organic matter; fertilisers provide concentrated plant nutrients.
- Crop rotation and legume cultivation can support soil fertility; Rhizobium fixes atmospheric nitrogen in association with legumes.
- Animal husbandry includes scientific care, breeding and management of livestock; apiculture = beekeeping, pisciculture = fish rearing, sericulture = silkworm rearing.
- Integrated pest management combines suitable methods and monitoring rather than relying only on repeated pesticide use.
- Store grains under suitable dry conditions and protect them from pests to reduce losses.

## 15. Verbal and Non-verbal Reasoning
- For number/letter series, compare differences, ratios, alternating patterns and squares/cubes.
- For coding, write each original letter and its transformation; do not skip repeated letters.
- Direction questions: draw a small compass and track each turn.
- In syllogisms, only select conclusions that logically must follow.
- For clock angles, minute hand moves 6° per minute; hour hand moves 0.5° per minute. At exact 3:00, the smaller angle is 90°.
- Check whether a question asks what *must* be true or what *may* be true.

## Common exam traps
1. Convert minutes to seconds before using m/s.
2. Do not confuse mass with weight, speed with velocity, or distance with displacement.
3. Write the formula first, substitute values, then check units.
4. In MCQs, estimate the result to catch arithmetic slips.
5. Read qualifiers such as “average”, “net”, “equal distance”, “neutral atom”, and “not”.
