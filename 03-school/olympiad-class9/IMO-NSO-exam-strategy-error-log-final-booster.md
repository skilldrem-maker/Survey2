# Class 9 IMO + NSO/ISO: Exam Strategy, Error Log & Final Booster

**Purpose:** Turn practice into marks. Use this alongside the chapter worksheets and challenge rounds in this folder. This guide is original study advice, not an official SOF paper.

## 1. The three-pass method

### Pass 1 — Secure marks
- Read the whole question carefully, including words such as **NOT**, **incorrect**, **minimum**, and **maximum**.
- Answer questions you can solve confidently without long calculations.
- Mark a doubtful question and move on; do not let one question consume the paper.

### Pass 2 — Solve and verify
- Return to marked questions.
- Write a short calculation or draw a diagram where useful.
- Check units, signs, arithmetic, and whether the answer matches the question.

### Pass 3 — Final scan
- Check that each response is marked in the correct question row.
- Revisit questions with negative signs, fractions, square roots, diagrams, or multi-step reasoning.
- Do not change an answer just because another option “looks right”; change it only when you find a reason.

## 2. IMO maths checklist

Before finalising a solution, ask:

- **Number systems:** Is the decimal terminating, recurring, or non-terminating? Did I simplify the fraction first?
- **Exponents and surds:** Did I apply exponent laws correctly? Is the square root exact or approximate?
- **Polynomials and identities:** Did I substitute carefully? Can factorisation or an identity shorten the work?
- **Coordinate geometry:** Did I use the correct x-coordinate first and y-coordinate second?
- **Linear equations:** Did I preserve equality when moving terms? Can I substitute the result back?
- **Lines and angles / triangles:** Does the diagram support the claim, or do I need a theorem?
- **Quadrilaterals and circles:** Which property or theorem justifies the step?
- **Heron’s formula / mensuration:** Are all lengths in the same unit? Is the final unit squared or cubed?
- **Statistics and probability:** Did I count all outcomes exactly once? Is the probability between 0 and 1?
- **Sequences:** Did I check the pattern using more than one term?

## 3. NSO/ISO science checklist

- **Physics:** Write the relevant formula first; convert units before substituting; check whether the answer is physically sensible.
- **Motion:** Distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity. Keep track of direction when needed.
- **Force and laws of motion:** Identify the interacting bodies and the direction of each force.
- **Gravitation and floatation:** Distinguish mass from weight and density from relative density.
- **Work, energy and power:** Check whether force acts along the displacement; use joules for work/energy and watts for power.
- **Sound:** Remember that sound needs a medium; distinguish frequency, amplitude, time period and speed.
- **Matter and atoms:** Keep particles, atoms, molecules, ions, elements and compounds distinct.
- **Cell biology:** Connect each organelle to its function; do not confuse plant-specific structures with structures found in both plant and animal cells.
- **Tissues and organisms:** Compare structure with function, not just names.
- **Environment and applied science:** Look for cause-and-effect relationships and eliminate options that contradict basic scientific principles.

## 4. Common traps to train against

| Trap | Better habit |
|---|---|
| Rushing past a key word | Circle or mentally emphasise the condition |
| Doing arithmetic in your head | Write the one line most likely to contain an error |
| Guessing from the diagram alone | Apply the relevant theorem or stated information |
| Forgetting units | Write units at every final step |
| Treating a familiar topic as an easy question | Solve what is asked, not what you expect |
| Changing answers repeatedly | Change only after finding a specific error |
| Memorising an answer key | Explain why the correct option works and why one tempting distractor fails |

## 6. Seven-day final booster

Use the time available each day; these are suggested tasks, not a requirement to study for long hours.

1. **Day 1 — Diagnose:** Take one timed mixed worksheet. Categorise each error.
2. **Day 2 — Repair:** Revise the two weakest maths sections; solve fresh questions without looking at answers.
3. **Day 3 — Science repair:** Revise the two weakest science sections; explain key ideas aloud in your own words.
4. **Day 4 — Mixed practice:** Complete a mixed IMO set and check every wrong or guessed answer.
5. **Day 5 — Science practice:** Complete a mixed NSO/ISO set; revisit formulas, units, diagrams and definitions.
6. **Day 6 — Mock and review:** Take a timed practice paper in a quiet setting, then spend time analysing mistakes.
7. **Day 7 — Light revision:** Use the formula/concept sheets and mistake log. Prioritise sleep and arrive prepared.

If you have more than seven days, repeat the cycle with different questions. If you have less time, focus on your error log and the weakest high-value concepts rather than trying to reread everything.
